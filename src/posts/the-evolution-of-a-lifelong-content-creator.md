It is actually wild to sit back and realize that I’ve been creating content for practically my entire life. Okay, well, almost my entire life, obviously I wasn't dropping videos as a newborn. But ever since elementary school, I knew I wanted to be a YouTuber or some kind of entertainer.

Back then, I’d pull up whatever recording software worked on my old, janky laptop, or even hijack the shared family laptop, just to record little videos. It was nothing crazy, just the goofy, innocent stuff you make as a kid. I’d record with my dad, my brother, or my friends—making silly faces, playing around, eating, or just having random conversations.

By the time middle school hit, I started recording way more screen footage. I got *Minecraft* for my birthday alongside a laptop, so I was gaming pretty hard every single day. I’d use Bandicam or CamStudio, software where you could only record for like 15 to 20 minutes at a time and it left a massive, ugly watermark across the top of the screen. But it was just fun fucking around and not caring about any of the technical stuff. I was literally recording just to record.

Sadly, I never posted any of those early gaming videos, and all of that footage is lost now. I still have a few old videos saved, not the screen recordings, but clips of me as a kid just talking directly to the camera.

### The High School "Get Rich Quick" Eras

Going into high school was when the dream got serious. I wanted to be a famous creator and get rich like PewDiePie, Markiplier, or CaptainSparklez.

That launched a era of trying literally *everything*:

* **DIY Content:** Posting tutorials on Instructables.
* **Tumblr:** Running a "just tiana things" page because being a Tumblr girl was so in back then.
* **Instagram:** Launching a million different niche accounts (to be honest, I still own half of them, while the rest got banned, deleted, or rebranded).
* **YouTube:** Uploading vlog-style videos because I didn't know what else to do to blow up fast.

Freshman year (or maybe late 8th grade), I decided to try Twitch. I made an account even though I had zero clue what I was doing. My first-ever stream was me playing *Minecraft* on a potato laptop, live-streaming the most choppy footage known to man. At one point, I saw the viewer count go up to "1" and I was so hyped. Then I realized... the stream tab was open on my own screen. I was my only viewer. The embarrassment was so real that I ended the stream right then and there and didn't touch Twitch again until 2020.

Honestly, wanting to stream full-time or do UGC so I don't have to work a traditional 9-to-5 collar job is highkey still the dream.

### 2020: The Streamer Boom & Unexpected Pivots

A lot of people jumped into streaming during 2020 because of quarantine when everyone was stuck at home gaming. It seems like I was just part of that wave, but for me, it was actually triggered by a sudden breakup. My ex broke up with me and kicked me out of the house in less than three hours on a random morning (like thanks for the warning, bro). If he and I had never broken up, I don't think I would have ever restarted streaming.

Out of every content creation side quest I've ever tried, streaming on Twitch is the only thing I've stayed relatively committed to.

It definitely hasn't been a smooth, linear rise:

* **The Highs:** I got to meet amazing people, grew a cozy little community, and went to TwitchCon two years in a row, which made me travel completely alone for the first time in my life.
* **The Taboo Dating Arc:** I dated one of my Twitch mods. It’s a total meme/taboo in the community for a reason, and damn, it really did not work out (though him being 10 years older probably didn't help).
* **The Viewership Dip:** Showing my boyfriend on stream cost me almost half my average viewers—dropping from an average of 13 viewers down to 7 just because I was loyal to one man.

Seeing other people's growth skyrocket while yours feels stagnant can leave a sour taste in your mouth. But 6 years is a long time, and I keep going because I genuinely love streaming for myself and for the community that has stuck by me through thick and thin.

### Where Things Stand Now

Right now, my mind and my room are both a mess, and everything feels a bit overwhelming. Between navigating life post-breakup, entering a new relationship, and dealing with the burnout of customer service work, my energy has been drained. Even though I'm currently between jobs, I get so tired that the urge to just bed-rot takes over, and getting out of bed to go live feels like a mountain. Plus, as a fully licensed esthetician working toward getting my own suite, figuring out how to balance building a client base with a stream schedule is a puzzle I'm still solving.

I've taken long breaks, but that doesn't mean I want to quit. Streaming has brought me some of my absolute best memories.

Writing all of this out at 3 AM running on fumes after a long day is weirdly therapeutic. This whole post is basically one massive late-night ramble, but at least it's raw. Thank you to everyone who has supported the journey so far, no matter what pivot comes next!
